Grow Continuity (Beta)

Each course is followed session by session: who came, who returned, who is still with the group, and who finished. Every number here is people, not rooms, unless a chapter says otherwise in so many words. Rock stays the record; this page only reads it, through reviewed read-only queries, and never changes anything. The seven colours are a chapter index and nothing more: each chapter carries one, in order, as the spine down its left edge, and the same seven sit under the title as a table of contents. The batch
Every number on this ladder is people. One person who checked in twice on one night counts once, which is why returned plus new is exactly what attended. The solid bar is the people who were also in the previous session. The hatched bar beside it is people who are new, or who came before, missed a session, and came back. The dashed hollow box after the bar is the people who were in the previous session and not this one: it is drawn as absence, not as a bar, because they are not in the room. The ink tick with the caret marks how many people have been in every session so far. Session 1 reads baseline: there is no previous session to compare it against, so it has no dropped box and no tick, and it is never written as a zero.

Did the people in session 2 come from session 1? Who is still with the group?

Completion shape
One person counts once here. This chapter and the ladder above both count people; what changes is the grain. The ladder reads one session at a time, and this reads the whole batch at once, so a person who came to three of four sessions appears on three rungs above and in exactly one bar here. Shares divide by the people the batch reached, never by attendance. A batch still running labels its top tier all so far, because nobody can have finished a session that has not happened.

Core pathway, right now
Build and Favor DNA sessions record no attendance, so this is not attendance against the board. It is the two places the same workflows write the same fact: the stage of a person's open request on the board, and the highest stage group they are an active member of. The two are meant to agree. A gap is a workflow that did not finish, not a person who did not come. A stage a pipeline does not have is a ghost question mark rather than an empty box, and never a zero. The two pipelines are never merged into one: they are different journeys with different stages.
